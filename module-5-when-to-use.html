Module 5: When to Use AI (& When Not To)

AI solves specific problems brilliantly. It fails spectacularly at others. How to tell the difference before you've invested six months.

The Hype vs. Reality

Everyone wants to use AI. "Can we use AI to..." is the default question. But AI isn't the answer to every problem.

Sometimes a database query is better. Sometimes a simple rule beats a model. Sometimes a human is faster.

Good Use Cases for AI

✓ Text Analysis & Categorization

Classify emails, receipts, support tickets, or content into categories.

Why it works: Clear categories. Fuzzy matching (typos, variations). Subjective boundaries.

Example: Categorize "Spent £45 at Tesco on groceries" → Groceries category.

✓ Summarization

Extract key information from long documents, emails, or transcripts.

Why it works: Subjective (what's "key" depends on context). Handles variability well.

Example: "Extract date, merchant, and amount from this receipt."

✓ Information Extraction

Pull structured data from unstructured sources (receipts, invoices, emails).

Why it works: Handles formatting variation. Learns from examples.

Example: Extract {date, merchant, amount, category} from a receipt photo.

✓ Content Generation (with Guardrails)

Generate personalized copy, emails, or explanations.

Why it works: Scale. Can't hire 100 copywriters. AI can generate 10K variations.

Important: Needs human review. Hallucinations are a risk.

✓ Semantic Search

Find similar documents, past conversations, or relevant examples.

Why it works: Understands meaning, not just keywords. "I lost my password" matches "forgot credentials".

Example: Customer support search — find past similar tickets.

Bad Use Cases for AI

✗ Exact Fact Lookup

Don't use AI if you need the exact answer. Example: "What's the company's Q2 revenue?"

Why it fails: Model will guess based on patterns. Just query the database.

✗ Deterministic Logic

Don't use AI for "if A then B" decisions. Example: "Apply 10% discount if order > £100".

Why it fails: AI is probabilistic. Use a rule engine. Faster, cheaper, reliable.

✗ Real-Time Decision Making Under Uncertainty

Don't use AI for instant, high-stakes decisions. Example: "Should we approve this loan application?"

Why it fails: AI will hallucinate. Needs human review. Defeats the speed advantage.

✗ Audio/Video Understanding (Today)

Vision models are getting better, but they still hallucinate on uncommon objects.

Why it fails: Real-world data is messier than training data. Reliability is low.

✗ Tasks With No Training Examples

AI learns from patterns in data. If you have zero examples, AI can't learn.

Why it fails: Will hallucinate. Use explicit rules instead.

The AI vs. Alternatives Decision Matrix

For a given problem, compare AI against alternatives:

AI
  • Handles fuzzy/variable input
  • Scales to 1M+ examples
  • Learns from examples
  • Cost: High (£0.01-1/request)
  • Latency: 2-10 seconds
Rule Engine
  • Fast (milliseconds)
  • Reliable (no hallucinations)
  • Cheap (once built)
  • Brittle (breaks on edge cases)
  • Hard to scale (adds 1 rule per edge case)
Database Query
  • Exact answers
  • Fast (< 100ms)
  • Cheap
  • Works only for structured data
  • No flexibility
Human Review
  • Perfect accuracy
  • Understands context
  • Expensive (£10-100/decision)
  • Slow (hours)
  • Doesn't scale

Decision Framework

  • Is the answer objective? → Use database or rules
  • Is the answer subjective? → Use AI
  • Do you have >100 examples? → AI might work
  • Do you have 0 examples? → Use rules or humans
  • Is speed critical (< 100ms)? → Not AI
  • Is accuracy critical (>99.9%)? → Not AI
  • Are costs low? → Maybe AI
  • Are costs high? → Definitely not AI
Real Metric: 78% of AI pilots die because they solved the wrong problem. Not because the model was bad, but because the problem didn't need AI in the first place.

The AI + Alternative Hybrid Approach

Best practice: Use AI where it excels. Use rules/databases for everything else.

Example: Expense Categorization

  • Merchant has known category? → Use database lookup
  • Amount > £5,000? → Manual review (high-value)
  • Merchant unknown? → AI categorization
  • AI confidence < 60%? → Flag for human review

Result: AI handles 70% of cases. Rules handle 20%. Humans handle 10%. Cheaper and more reliable than AI-only.

Your "When to Use AI" Checklist

  • Define the problem precisely (what are you solving?)
  • Check if it's objective or subjective
  • Count: Do you have >100 training examples?
  • Check alternatives: database, rules, humans
  • Compare cost: AI vs. alternative
  • Compare latency: AI vs. alternative
  • Compare accuracy: AI vs. alternative
  • Estimate: How long will this take to build?
  • Ask: Is AI actually the best solution here?
Final Insight: The best AI projects aren't the ones with the fanciest models. They're the ones that solved the right problem with the right tool. Sometimes the right tool isn't AI.
