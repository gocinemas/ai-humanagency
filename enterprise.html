Enterprise AI Framework

A framework for taking AI from a novelty to something that works inside an enterprise: from embedding it in daily workflows, through process redesign, oversight and governance.

Overview

Seven layers, roughly in the order a company meets them. Most AI projects that stall are missing one of these, usually process redesign or evaluation.

The 7 layers

1

Intelligence to the workflows

Embed AI directly into how work already gets done, rather than as a bolt-on tool.

2

Re-engineer the process

Don't just automate the old process. Redesign it around what AI now makes possible.

3

Aggregate the right content

Pull together the right sources and context the AI needs to be useful.

4

Right human in the loop

Place oversight where it actually matters, not everywhere by default.

5

Drive change management

Adoption is a people problem as much as a technical one.

6

Domain-specific evals

Generic benchmarks aren't enough. Evaluation has to be built for the specific domain.

7

Security and governance of data and process

Keep data handling and process control tight as AI touches more of the operation.

Horizontal or vertical

As these layers mature, they split into two kinds of providers:

Horizontal

Cross-industry infrastructure that any enterprise can use.

Vertical

Built for one industry's workflows and content.

How an AI agent is built

An agent is an LLM with three parts wrapped around it.

1. The LLM: the brain

The non-deterministic core that everything else wraps around.

2a. Tools

Calendar, documents, accounts. Chosen according to the user's intent.

2b. Knowledge and memory

Knowledge feeds RAG. Memory has a short-term (semantic) and long-term (episodic) part.

3. Evals: the checks

  • An AI judge, scored against a rubric
  • Start by defining what "good" looks like before building the checks

Retrieval (RAG)

  • A shared folder of documents is the source
  • Retrieval pulls the relevant content
  • That content augments and grounds the LLM's answer
